# Creative Brief for Design

Ad creative is slow and expensive to redo. A vague brief produces generic work and a round of revisions; a precise one produces usable creative the first time and gives the team clean variants to test. The brief must contain every decision the designer would otherwise have to guess, while leaving room for craft.

## Principle: one message, one action, every spec included
An ad that tries to say three things says nothing. Each brief commits to one key message for one audience with one desired action, and lists every technical requirement so nothing is discovered at delivery.

## Inputs
- Campaign goal, audience, funnel stage, offer, and landing page
- Approved ad copy and angles (from the copy skills), compliance flags resolved
- `clients/<slug>/context.md`: brand guidelines location (logo files, colors, typefaces, imagery rules), restrictions, required disclosures
- Existing creative performance (top performers, fatigued ads), competitor creative from the swipe file
- Available assets: photography, video footage, product shots, testimonials with permissions

## Brief structure
1. **Objective and context.** Business goal, campaign, platform and placements, and why this creative is needed now (new campaign, refresh of fatigued ads, test).
2. **Audience.** Who sees this and what they are thinking when they scroll past it; their main pain, desire, or objection.
3. **Single key message and desired action.** One sentence each. Plus the proof point supporting the message, which must be substantiated.
4. **Concept and hook.** The creative idea in a few sentences, the visual or opening-second hook that earns attention, and why it fits the audience. For video, the first three seconds are specified separately.
5. **Visual direction.** Style, mood, color use within brand, composition priorities (subject first, text safe zones), imagery to use or avoid (people, locations, products, realism over stock gloss), and 2 to 3 reference examples with notes on what to take from each. Reference the brand guide rather than restating it.
6. **Copy on creative.** Exact on-image text (headline, subline, call to action), character or word limits, text-to-image balance guidance for the platform, and the placement of legal lines.
7. **Required elements.** Logo placement, license numbers or disclosures, phone number or URL, review stars or badges only if substantiated and permitted, and any mandatory terms from the context file.
8. **Specifications per placement.** Table of formats, aspect ratios, pixel dimensions, safe zones (for example Stories and Reels top and bottom margins), file types, file size limits, video length, frame rate, captions or subtitles requirement (assume sound off), thumbnail frame, and audio notes. Verify against current platform specs and list the date checked.
9. **Video-specific notes.** Script or shot list, voiceover or on-screen text, pacing, music licensing requirements, subtitle burn-in, end card with call to action.
10. **Variants for testing.** The list of variants to produce (angles, hooks, formats), what differs between them and what stays constant, and naming for each file matching the campaign naming convention.
11. **Compliance and permissions.** Model releases, testimonial permission, music and stock licenses, platform restrictions relevant to the industry.
12. **Assets and delivery.** Links to brand files, source footage, fonts; delivery formats; where to upload; naming; deadline with review dates; who approves; number of revision rounds included.
13. **Success criteria.** What good looks like (the hook readable in one second at feed size, the message understood without sound) and how performance will be judged after launch.

## Output
Save to `clients/<slug>/outputs/YYYY-MM/creative-brief-<campaign>.md` with a specs table; when the team works in a design tool, also provide a short version suitable for pasting into a task. Offer to place the brief or specs in the design workflow.
```
Bottom line (2 sentences): what is being made and why.
The brief in the structure above.
Open questions for the account manager, marked clearly (with a default assumption for each so work can proceed).
```

## Rules
- Do not specify creative that would break platform policy (misleading before and after imagery, prohibited content, sensitive attribute implications).
- Do not request likenesses of real people or copyrighted material without permission documented.
- Keep to one message per ad; push extra messages into separate variants.
- Treat specs as time-sensitive: platforms change dimensions and safe zones, so state the verification date.
