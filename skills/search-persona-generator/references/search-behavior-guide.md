# Search behavior guide

Read this when filling the "How they search" section. It turns a persona's motivation into the
query and SERP detail the SEO team needs. Keep it practical; the goal is queries a strategist can
drop straight into a keyword tool.

## 1. Choosing personas (before any queries)

Personas are split by **motivation and buying situation**, not by demographics. Two 40-year-old
homeowners are different personas if one has a broken furnace and the other is planning a
replacement. Work through the site's services and ask, for each: *what situation puts someone in
front of a search box wanting this?* Group the answers; each distinct group is a candidate.

Then cap at three using this priority:
1. The situation that drives the most revenue or the most leads (usually the core service).
2. The situation with the longest research phase (most content opportunity).
3. A secondary segment the site visibly targets (commercial, B2B, a second audience, a niche).

Merge candidates that would search almost identically. If the site is a single-service brand,
one or two personas is correct; do not pad to three.

Typical splits by business type:

| Business type | Common persona axes |
|---|---|
| Local services (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, legal, dental) | emergency vs planned vs commercial / property manager |
| Healthcare & wellness | symptom-driven patient vs caregiver / parent vs second-opinion seeker |
| B2B services & agencies | owner-operator vs in-house manager vs procurement / evaluator |
| SaaS / software | end user with a pain vs team lead comparing vs executive buying |
| E-commerce | problem-solver (needs X) vs gift buyer vs repeat / loyal buyer |
| Education & training | prospective student vs parent vs career changer |

## 2. Intent buckets

Fill each bucket with 3-5 queries per persona. Lower case, as typed, no quotes.

| Bucket | What the searcher wants | Query shape | Example patterns |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Informational** | Understand the problem or option | symptom, "how to", "what is", "why" | `ac blowing warm air`, `what is a manual j calculation` |
| **Commercial** | Compare providers or solutions | "best", "top", "reviews", "vs", `{service} {city}`, "near me" | `best hvac company bayport`, `heat pump vs furnace` |
| **Transactional** | Take action now | "book", "quote", "cost", "24 hour", "same day", "buy", "pricing", "schedule" | `same day ac repair near me`, `heat pump installation quote` |
| **Question** | Natural-language question | full question a person would ask a search engine or an AI assistant | `how much does ac repair cost in ny`, `are heat pumps worth it in new york` |

Rules of thumb:
- Head terms (2 words) and long-tail (5+ words) in every bucket.
- Local businesses: include the service area in at least one commercial and one transactional query.
- Use the site's own vocabulary where a customer would also use it; translate jargon otherwise
  ("ductless mini-split" stays, "Manual J" becomes "how is ac sized").
- Question queries feed FAQ blocks, People Also Ask targeting and answer-engine optimization.
  Write them so a one-paragraph answer could satisfy them.
- Never duplicate a query across buckets; pick the bucket that fits its dominant intent.

## 3. Modifiers

List the modifiers the persona habitually adds. Common sets:

- **Urgency:** emergency, 24 hour, same day, near me, open now, today
- **Price:** cost, price, cheap, affordable, pricing, how much, free estimate, financing
- **Quality:** best, top rated, reviews, licensed, certified, trusted
- **Comparison:** vs, versus, alternative, or, difference between
- **Specificity:** brand names, model names, materials, sizes
- **Audience / use case:** for small business, for seniors, for kids, commercial, residential
- **Location:** city, neighborhood, county, "near me", zip

## 4. SERP features by persona type

| Persona type | Features they actually click |
|---|---|
| Emergency / urgent local | Local pack, call button, Google Business Profile reviews, ads |
| Planned / research-heavy | People Also Ask, organic guides, video, AI overviews, comparison articles |
| B2B / procurement | Organic service pages, directories and listings, LinkedIn, case studies |
| E-commerce shopper | Shopping results, product carousels, review snippets |
| Healthcare patient | Local pack, knowledge panels, PAA, authoritative health sites |

Name the features so the team knows what to optimize: reviews for local pack personas, FAQ
schema for question-heavy personas, video for how-to personas.

## 5. Funnel stage definitions

- **awareness** - knows the symptom, not the solution. Mostly informational queries.
- **consideration** - knows the solution category, comparing options or providers.
- **decision** - ready to book, buy or call. Transactional and local queries dominate.
- **mixed** - researches and decides in the same session (emergencies that still check
  reviews, B2B evaluators). Use sparingly.

## 6. Device and context

State the device *and* the moment: "mobile, standing in a hot kitchen", "desktop, during work
hours, with a spreadsheet of vendors open". It changes what the landing page must do first
(click-to-call vs detailed comparison).

## 7. From persona to page

Each persona should map to at least one page the site has or should have:

| Persona stage | Page types that convert them |
|---|---|
| awareness | symptom / problem guides, "what is" explainers, checklists |
| consideration | service pages with proof, comparison guides, cost guides, case studies |
| decision | service + city pages, pricing / financing pages, booking page, emergency page |

Content gaps are the pages a persona needs that the profile shows no evidence of. List them
plainly in the "How the SEO team should use this" section; they are the first things a
strategist will act on.
