# Format rules by page type

Load the section(s) for the page type in the brief. A page can combine types (for example a comparison article with an FAQ).

## FAQ sections

- Heading `## Frequently Asked Questions`, each question an H3 phrased the way a customer types or says it ("Do I need a water softener for a tankless water heater in Portland?").
- Each answer is a complete, standalone statement of 2-4 sentences with a specific number or detail. Never a one-line "Yes, we do."
- Do not repeat a question already answered under its own H2; FAQ questions are the extra ones.

## Comparison content (X vs Y)

- Open with one sentence that states the single most important comparative fact, with specifics, and names both entities.
- Follow with a table. Header row names both entities (`| Feature | Tankless water heater | Tank water heater |`). Each row is one attribute, with the figure and source in the cell.
- Every comparative sentence names both entities.
- End with a "which should I choose" question heading that says who each option suits, with the deciding numbers.

## How-to / process content

- A question H2 ("How do you descale a tankless water heater?"), then numbered H3 steps or a numbered list.
- Each step starts with an imperative verb (Shut off, Close, Attach, Flush, Restore), not a transition word (First, Then, Next, After that).
- Each step makes sense on its own: name the part or tool again rather than "it".
- Call out safety-critical steps in their own line, starting with **Safety:** and saying what to do and what happens if skipped. Never bury a safety step mid-sentence.
- State time, tools and cost up front when known.

## Definition / "What is X" content

- The core definition is one sentence directly under the heading.
- That sentence gives the defining mechanism, not just a category ("A HELOC is a revolving line of credit secured by your home's equity that you draw on as needed", not "A HELOC is a type of loan").
- Where natural, the same or next sentence contrasts it with the familiar alternative ("unlike a home equity loan, which pays one lump sum").
- Follow with how it works, then the how much, how long and what if questions.

## Listicles / "Best X" / "Top N"

- Each item heading is a numbered H3 naming the item and its specific differentiator or ideal use ("1. Rinnai RE199iN: best for homes with two showers running at once"), never "Great option" or "Top pick".
- Each item has at least one concrete spec or number that justifies its place, with its source.
- If two items serve the same use, say so rather than inventing a distinction.
- Add a summary table of all items (name, best for, key spec, price) when there are 3 or more.

## Product / service pages

- Show a specific price, price range or "starting at $X" in visible text when the brief or client files give one. Unknown price is `[NEEDS INPUT: starting price]`, never a guess.
- State availability, service area and eligibility as explicit facts ("We serve Portland, Beaverton and Lake Oswego"), not "in most cases" or "typically available".
- The CTA states the exact next action, the contact route the brief gives, and what happens after.
