# YouTube Benchmarking for Video-Heavy Competitors

Use when a competitor invests in video. The goal is to learn which topics and packaging earn reach, not to optimize the user's own videos.

## Why it matters

Google surfaces YouTube results in the main SERP, video mode, and Discover, so a ranking video earns two surfaces at once. YouTube also appears heavily in AI answers: per the OtterlyAI YouTube Citation Study (2026), YouTube and Reddit together account for roughly 78% of social-media citations in AI Overviews, and YouTube citations rose ~25% since Jan 2025, led by instructional and visual-demo content, with long-form videos dominating. Treat these as reported figures, not guarantees.

## Outlier method

1. List the competitor channel's recent videos (public channel page, or a user-pasted export) with views and publish dates.
2. Compute the channel average views over the sample. Show the arithmetic and label it Calculated.
3. Flag **outliers**: videos with views >= 2x the channel average.
4. For each outlier, record topic, format (tutorial / review / vlog / Shorts), length, title, thumbnail approach, and whether chapters and captions exist.
5. Look for repeated patterns across outliers (topics, framing, length). Those are the lessons to carry into the Strengths to Learn From section.

## Packaging checklist (what to look for in competitor wins)

**Title**: under ~60 characters (mobile truncates beyond that); primary keyword in the first few words; leads with the payoff or a curiosity gap in plain language; no keyword stuffing or ALL CAPS beyond one emphasized word.

**Thumbnail**: high contrast, bold readable type legible at thumbnail size; one clear subject, uncluttered; complements rather than repeats the title text; custom thumbnails are near-universal among top videos.

**Description**: primary and secondary keyword in the first 2-3 lines (the visible snippet); plain-language summary; relevant links only; timestamped chapters.

**Chapters and transcripts**: chapters start at `0:00`, at least three, each >=10s, titled in searchable words; accurate uploaded captions rather than auto-captions. These are the text search engines and AI systems read and quote.

**Hook and length**: the payoff is stated in the first ~15-30 seconds; long-form tends to dominate AI citations for instructional topics, while Shorts handle discovery.

**Tags**: a minor signal; do not credit long tag lists as a strength.

CTR and average view duration are the signals that compound. If the user pastes their own YouTube Studio data (CTR, average view duration, traffic sources), compare it against the competitor patterns and label it User-provided.
