# Regulatory starters

Candidates for `## Regulatory Framework` in compliance.md. Pick by what the client does, where it sells and which channels it uses. Keep only rules that apply, say why, and confirm current status with a targeted search when web access exists. Without web access, mark time-sensitive items `verify before publishing`.

## Applies to almost every US business that markets

- FTC Act Section 5 (unfair or deceptive claims): claims need substantiation before publishing.
- FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255): testimonials, influencer posts and reviews; disclose material connections; typical results.
- FTC Trade Regulation Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465): no fake, bought or suppressed reviews.
- CAN-SPAM Act: commercial email needs a working unsubscribe, a postal address and honest subject lines.
- TCPA: SMS and autodialed calls need prior express written consent.
- ADA Title III and WCAG 2.1 AA: accessible web content (alt text, contrast, captions).

## By industry

| Industry | Bodies and rules to consider |
| :--- | :--- |
| Healthcare, therapy, dental, medical | HIPAA Privacy Rule (patient testimonials need a written authorization; PHI never in marketing tools), HHS OCR guidance on tracking technologies, the state licensing board's advertising rules (titles, credentials, "specialist" use), Medicare marketing limits when the client bills Medicare, FTC health-claim substantiation |
| Supplements, food, beverage, cosmetics | FDA labeling and health or structure-function claims, USDA Organic (only certified operations may say organic), FTC Green Guides for environmental claims, country-of-origin and "Made in USA" standard (FTC) |
| Financial services, lending, investing | SEC Marketing Rule, FINRA Rule 2210, CFPB and UDAAP, Truth in Lending (Regulation Z) trigger terms, state lending licenses, "not FDIC insured" disclosures |
| Insurance | State insurance department advertising rules, NAIC model advertising rules |
| Legal | State bar advertising rules (ABA Model Rules 7.1 to 7.3), "attorney advertising" labels, no guaranteed outcomes |
| Real estate, mortgage | Fair Housing Act advertising, RESPA, state license numbers in ads, NMLS ID for mortgage |
| Subscriptions and memberships | ROSCA, FTC Negative Option Rule, state automatic renewal laws (for example California ARL): clear terms before billing, express consent, easy online cancellation |
| Ecommerce shipping across borders | Canada Competition Act (misleading representations, drip pricing), Quebec Charter of the French Language for Quebec buyers, EU Consumer Rights Directive for EU buyers |
| Software, SaaS, data | GDPR and UK GDPR when serving EU or UK users, CCPA and CPRA, cookie consent (ePrivacy and PECR), SOC 2 or ISO claims only with a current report |
| Contests, giveaways, referral programs | State sweepstakes laws (no purchase necessary), FTC disclosure of referral rewards, CASL limits on referral emails in Canada |

## By geography and channel

- Email or SMS to Canada: CASL (express or implied consent, identification, unsubscribe within 10 business days).
- EU or UK audience: GDPR, ePrivacy or PECR for cookies and email.
- California consumers: CCPA and CPRA notices, California ARL for subscriptions.
- Paid social or search ads: platform ad policies for the industry (health, finance, alcohol and others are restricted).
