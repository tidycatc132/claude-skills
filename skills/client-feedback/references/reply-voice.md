# Reply voice

The reply goes out from the Account Manager in WISE Digital Partners' voice: the agency talking to its client. The homepage copy is in the client's brand voice; none of its slogans or style belong in the reply.

## What the reply does, in this order

1. Greets the client by first name (`Hi Lena,`) on the first line (**checked**).
2. Thanks them in one plain sentence and says what we heard: what they approved and which design they chose, if any.
3. Says what we will change, grouped (copy changes, then design changes), in a sentence or two each. No need to repeat every item.
4. Answers each question. When the answer depends on something not in the files (a delivery date, a launch date), say what it depends on and that the AM will confirm it, without inventing a date or promising a deadline.
5. Scope: for every request outside the scope source or still to confirm, say plainly that it goes beyond what the current agreement covers, that the AM will send a change order, quote or estimate for it (name one of those three, **checked**), and that work on the agreed pages carries on meanwhile. A sentence about pages may not agree to add or build them (**checked**).
6. Access and files: thank them for what they sent; if they sent a login or password, ask them to invite WISE as a user instead and suggest they change that password. Never repeat it, and never ask anyone to send a password (**checked**).
7. Closes with the next step and when they will hear from us next, only as precisely as the files allow.

The script adds the sign-off (the AM's name and `Account Manager, WISE Digital Partners`); leave it out of the body (**checked**).

## How it sounds

- "I" for the AM, "we" for WISE. Short paragraphs of one to three sentences. At most 300 words (**checked**).
- Warm, direct and specific. Agree with what we will do; be clear and kind about what needs a decision.
- Plain text: no headings, bold or bullets unless listing three or more changes.
- Every number in the reply, dates included, comes from the client's text or the kickoff context (**checked**). Never set a delivery or launch date yourself.

## Never

- Stock agency phrases (**checked**): thrilled, delighted, excited, don't hesitate, do not hesitate, world-class, best-in-class, rest assured, as per, per our, touch base, circle back, no problem, absolutely, guarantee, synergy.
- Internal labels in the client's email (**checked**): `GAP`, `CONFLICT`, `SCOPE`, R numbers, "scope source", "change log", "intake item", "updates file", "client context", "feedback table".
- Promises of rankings, traffic or a launch date nobody has set.
- Agreeing to extra pages or deliverables, even warmly ("we can definitely add those").
