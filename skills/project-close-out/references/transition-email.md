# Transition email

Load this before writing `closeout/<slug>/<slug>-transition-email.md`. Each rule names the `check` row that tests it.

## Shape

The first four lines are the header the scaffold wrote (`Client:`, `To:`, `Launch:`, `Account manager:`); leave them as they are (check: email header). Then a blank line, `Subject: ` and a plain subject that celebrates the launch with the client's name, for example "Larkspur Family Dental is live" (check: email subject).

The body runs from the greeting to the sign-off, under 300 words (check: email length). Aim for 170 to 240. In this order:

1. `Hi <first name>,` (check: greeting). The first name is the first word of the `To:` line.
2. **Celebrate the launch.** One or two sentences naming the launch date in words, such as "went live on November 20" (check: launch date), and one thing specific to this client: their services, towns or the kind of patients or customers the site is for.
3. **What launched, 3 to 5 bullets.** Each bullet leads with or contains a real number from the scaffold's counts: pages (total, or a page-type count next to its own type: "7 service pages"), words of new copy, old addresses redirected (checks: bullets, numbers, page counts, word count stated). Say what the number means for the client, in plain words.
4. **The next three months**, in a short paragraph, using the words "the next three months" (check: next three months). Turn the roadmap's Month 1 to 3 tasks into plain outcomes: Google finding every page, tracking set up, small launch fixes finished, new guides on questions their customers ask, reviews or local links. Name no tool, task id or internal label. Each outcome must be one a roadmap task actually names: "visits are being measured" when a task confirms analytics, but not calls, leads or bookings unless a task says so. Without a roadmap, say the plan for the next three months follows with the first monthly report, and name nothing in it.
5. **Reporting and check-ins.** The rhythm the scaffold printed (default: a monthly report and a monthly check-in call), fitted to the client's stated preferences where the context has them (check: reporting cadence).
6. **Main contact.** The Account Manager is their main contact from now on, and how to reach them (reply to this email is enough) (check: main contact).
7. A one-line thank-you in the client's voice.
8. The sign-off as the last lines: the AM's name on its own line when given, then `Account Manager, WISE Digital Partners`; with no name, that line alone (check: sign-off).

## Words

- Follow the Email row of the voice file the scaffold printed. With none: plain, warm, short sentences, no exclamation marks.
- Numbers: only the scaffold's counts, parts of the launch date, months 1 to 3, and numbers in the names of the roadmap's Month 1 to 3 tasks (not their descriptions, which carry competitor and search figures), the cadence, the context's communication lines or the go-live email's launch line (check: numbers). No percentages, rankings, traffic or lead promises.
- Never use: QA, BugHerd, severities (critical, major, minor), GO or NO-GO, handoff, deferred, carry-over, seo-drift-monitor, keyword tiers, SOW, scope change, technical debt, dev index or ClickUp, or a finding id (check: no internal terms).
- Never claim structured data, schema, rich results, markup or star ratings in search (check: no structured data), even when the PM asks: say in your answer that the process does not include it.
- Name live chat or Juvo only when the go-live email's `Integrations:` line names it (check: only named integrations).
- The only link allowed is the live site, on its own domain (check: links).
- Keep open issues in plain, light words ("a couple of small fixes from launch"); never list them.
