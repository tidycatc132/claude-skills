# Writing guide

The case study is public: a prospect reads it on the WISE site or in a pitch. It is written in WISE's voice (first person plural, "we"), not the client's. The design brief is internal, for the designer making the showcase assets.

## Narrative slots

- **Headline.** One line naming the client and the concrete change, for example "Kestrel Garage Doors: a phone-first site that sends repair calls to the right page". No question headlines, no hype, no figure the script did not count.
- **Client Snapshot.** The script fills the facts. Add two sentences from the context's Snapshot and Business Overview: what the business does, where, and for whom.
- **The Challenge.** What the old site lacked and why it hurt the business: the context's `## Website Today`, the Growth Blueprint's Business and Market Overview and, if one is approved, the client's own words. Two short paragraphs at most. Only problems a file states.
- **The Approach.** The choices we made and why: positioning from the Blueprint's Strategic Positioning (or the kit's brand.md Core Positioning and Key Differentiators), and the order of work from Growth Strategy Phase 1. Name the pages that carried each choice.
- **What We Built.** Keep the script's bullets. Add one short paragraph naming specific pages and what each does for the client.
- **Results.** At launch the script writes the opening sentence; add what will be measured at 90 days, from the Blueprint's Measurement and Next Steps (calls, forms, search clicks to named pages). With a metrics file, report each figure as before and after, exactly as the file has it, and say what period is compared. Do not turn figures into percentages, multiples or totals.
- **In Their Words.** With approved quotes, keep one as a `> "..."` line, word for word, then `— Speaker, Business`. Without, keep the `QUOTE NEEDED:` line and write one open question for the AM to ask the named contact, ending in a question mark; it should invite a specific answer (what changed in the office, what customers say on the phone).
- **Quick Stats.** 4 to 6 bullets, each led by one of the script's counts (or a metrics-file figure at 90 days). Reword the text after the number if it reads better; never the number.

## Design brief slots

- **Callout Stats.** 3 to 6 bullets (3 or 4 reads best), copied from the narrative's Quick Stats.
- **Layout Suggestion.** A short paragraph: the hero shot (which page, which device), where the before/after pairs sit, how the callout stats are set in the brand colours from the table, and where the quote or its slot goes.

## Voice

- Specific beats general: "a page each for Meridian and Nampa" beats "local SEO pages".
- Plain verbs: built, wrote, moved, added. Avoid hype words (game-changing, cutting-edge, world-class, seamless, skyrocket, transformative and the like); the check lists the ones it catches.
- No promises: no rankings, traffic or lead numbers the files do not state, and no "will" claims about results.
- No internal terms: QA, BugHerd, SOW, keyword tiers, file names or paths, GO/NO-GO.
- No structured data, schema or rich result claims: they are not part of the process.
